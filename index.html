      How it works

      Every Division I team gets one number: how many points better or worse it is than an average team on a neutral court. A team with a rating of +20 should beat a +5 team by about 15.

      The ratings come from a PageRank-style model, the same idea Google used to rank web pages. Every game splits a vote between the two teams. The winner's share grows with the square root of the margin, after taking out home court, so a 20-point win counts for more than a 5-point win but not four times as much. Margins are capped at 50, so running up the score doesn't pay.

      Beating a good team is worth more than beating a bad one, because good teams have more rating to pass along. The whole season is re-solved from scratch every morning.

      Turning ratings into a line

      • Rating gap between the two teams, in points
      • Plus 3 points for the home team
      • Plus a small travel adjustment for long road trips

      Win probability assumes the final margin lands within a normal spread of about 11.8 points around the prediction, which is how far off a typical game finishes.

      Injuries

      Each morning the model reads the college basketball injury report from Covers. A player's value is how much his team's margin drops when he sits, estimated from his box-score production (Game Score) and calibrated against more than 1,400 real absences over two seasons. Role players are worth close to nothing, because the bench absorbs their minutes; a star is worth three to five points.

      Out counts in full, doubtful 75%, questionable 50% and probable 10%. If a player has already been missing, the ratings already reflect games without him, so only the share of games he actually played is counted. A player who has sat all season doesn't move the line at all.

      Reading the games page

      Model is the predicted spread. Market is the betting line when one is posted. Edge is the gap between the two, and the highlighted team is the side the model prefers against that line.

      Predictions are made each morning before tip-off and are never revised after the fact. Every graded result on the Record page comes from that morning's numbers.

      For fun and analysis. Not betting advice.
